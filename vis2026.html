AccessViz: 3rd Workshop on Accessible Data Visualization

Chart-Supported or Model-Supplied? Examining MLLM-Generated Claims for Accessible Visualization

Ishrat Jahan Eliza, Md Dilshadur Rahman

Multimodal language models are now used to describe charts for blind and low-vision readers, but it is not always clear where their statements come from. We examined 1,224 descriptions of 102 charts from three models, labeling each claim as stated in the chart, derived from it, or speculative. Adding data tables, captions, and alt text made claims more grounded; explanations of real-world significance stayed mostly speculative.

Presented by Ishrat Jahan Eliza

BELIV: Learning What’s True, Doing What’s Right

Guidelines Are Not Rules: Characterizing Terminologies around Visualization Design Guidelines

Anna L. Chinni, Md Dilshadur Rahman, Bon Adriel Aseniero, Petra Isenberg, Kushin Mukherjee, Ghulam Jilani Quadri, Paul Rosen, Karen B. Schloss, Daniel Weiskopf

Visualization research often presents its findings as “guidelines,” yet researchers read that word in different ways. Drawing on an expert study, a crowdsourced study, and the terminology of 3,877 IEEE VIS papers, we examine how the community understands it alongside related terms such as heuristic, best practice, and rule of thumb.

Presented by Anna L. Chinni and Md Dilshadur Rahman

A Survey on Annotations in Information Visualization: Empirical Studies, Applications, and Challenges

Md Dilshadur Rahman, Bhavana Doppalapudi, Ghulam Jilani Quadri, Paul Rosen

A survey of 191 papers on annotation in visualization. We classify annotations by type, by how they are created, and by what they refer to, and review their role in engagement, storytelling, collaboration, and exploratory analysis. All surveyed papers can be explored in an interactive browser.

Presented by Md Dilshadur Rahman

Cluster: The Practitioners’ Summit · Talk

Designing Annotations in Visualization: Considerations from Visualization Practitioners and Educators

Md Dilshadur Rahman, Devin Lange, Ghulam Jilani Quadri, Paul Rosen

Drawing on interviews with ten practitioners and seven educators, this talk looks at how professionals decide what to annotate, where to place it, and how much to include. We distill their practice into six design considerations, each paired with published examples. The paper appeared in Computer Graphics Forum (EuroVis 2026).

Presented by Md Dilshadur Rahman
